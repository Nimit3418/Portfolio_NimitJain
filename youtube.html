Building My YouTube Channel Alongside College

I didn’t start YouTube with a clear plan to become a content creator. It started much more naturally - with questions.

Before starting college, I had a lot of doubts about what I should learn, what actually matters in the first year, which mistakes I could avoid, and how I could prepare myself better. While figuring these things out for myself, I realised that many other students were asking the same questions.

That became the reason I started making videos.

My goal was simple: share the things I was learning in a way that I would have found useful before starting college. Instead of making generic advice videos, I wanted to research topics properly, add my own experience where relevant, and make content that students could actually use.

What started small gradually became a serious project.

⸻

From an Idea to the Entire Process

One thing I didn’t expect when I started was how much goes into making a single video.

I handle almost everything myself.

It usually starts with finding a topic worth making. I research it from different sources, understand the subject, decide what is actually useful for students, and then structure everything into a script.

After that comes recording, editing, creating the thumbnail, figuring out the title and description, adding chapters where needed, and finally publishing it.

Then comes another part that I find equally interesting - looking at what happened after publishing.

I check how people responded, where viewers dropped off, whether the thumbnail and title worked, what people commented, and what I could change in the next video.

So the process has gradually become:

Research → Plan → Script → Record → Edit → Package → Publish → Analyse → Improve

I’ve learned something from almost every stage.

⸻

Doing It Alongside College

The biggest challenge has been doing all of this while being a student.

YouTube doesn’t operate on a college timetable. A video might require several hours of research and editing just when I have assignments, exams, projects, or other things to work on.

There have been times when I had an idea ready but couldn’t work on it because academics had to come first. There have also been videos that took much more time than I expected.

And then there’s the frustrating part: sometimes you put a lot of work into a video and it simply doesn’t perform.

I’ve experienced that too.

But I’ve realised that this is part of building something independently. I can’t control how every video performs. What I can control is whether I learn from it.

⸻

Learning From What Doesn’t Work

Some videos have done well. Some haven’t.

Instead of treating the numbers as just a success-or-failure metric, I’ve started using them as feedback.

A video that doesn’t get the response I expected makes me question the entire process:

  • Was the topic actually relevant?
  • Was the title clear?
  • Did the thumbnail give someone a reason to click?
  • Was the introduction too slow?
  • Was the video unnecessarily long?
  • Did I explain the topic properly?

These questions have changed the way I make content.

I’ve learned that creating something useful isn’t enough by itself. People first need to understand why they should spend their time watching it.

That has made me much more conscious about communication, presentation, storytelling, and the viewer’s perspective.

⸻

The Numbers

Over time, the channel started reaching people beyond my immediate circle.

Today, it has crossed:

  • 43K+ views
  • 500+ subscribers
  • 1,700+ hours of watch time

Those numbers are a nice way to measure progress, but the part that means more to me is the feedback from students.

I’ve had 38+ students tell me that my guidance helped them with their college application or decision-making.

That’s when the channel started feeling different to me.

A view is a number on a dashboard. Knowing that someone actually used something I researched and explained makes the work feel tangible.

It also influences what I choose to make next.

⸻

What YouTube Has Taught Me

YouTube has ended up teaching me much more than how to make videos.

  • Research taught me how to find information, compare sources, and filter out what isn’t useful.
  • Scripting taught me to organise my thoughts instead of simply knowing things.
  • Recording pushed me to communicate more clearly.
  • Editing taught me attention to detail and patience.
  • Thumbnails and titles taught me that even good work needs good presentation.
  • Analytics taught me to accept when something isn’t working.
  • And publishing taught me something simple: you can’t improve a video that never gets made.

I’ve also become much more comfortable with experimentation. A video doesn’t have to be perfect for me to publish it. It needs to be good enough to put into the world, learn from, and improve upon.

⸻

Building Something of My Own

The channel is still a work in progress.

There are older videos where I can immediately see things I’d do differently today - the way I would structure the script, edit the video, design the thumbnail, or even choose the topic.

But I don’t see that as wasted work.

Each video is a snapshot of what I knew at that point, and each new upload gives me an opportunity to improve.

That’s probably what I value most about the channel.

It started because I wanted to answer questions that I had as a student. Along the way, it became a place where I could research, create, experiment, make mistakes, and build something independently while continuing my education.

I don’t know exactly where the channel will go from here.

For now, I’m still doing what started the whole thing:

Learn something → turn it into something useful → put it out there → learn from the response → do it better next time.
