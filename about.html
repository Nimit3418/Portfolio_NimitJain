"I didn't start coding to win hackathons. I started because I had questions nobody would answer."

I arrived at Vedam School of Technology from Delhi looking for answers about college life, tech stacks, and what actually mattered. When I couldn't find them, I started a YouTube channel to document my search. That channel became a community of 38+ students-and it taught me my first real lesson in building.

The Creator

Video production, visual design, and scripting aren't distractions from engineering; they are the foundation of product thinking. Building an audience taught me that the best code in the world doesn't matter if you can't communicate its value.

Reaching the right people is just as critical as shipping the right feature. That realization shifted how I approach every project.

The Engineer

Answering questions was a start, but building systems was the answer. I moved from content creation to full-stack engineering, co-founding CampusCritique to solve the very problems I was talking about.

My stack evolved aggressively: React, Next.js, and Supabase for the frontend, Node.js and Express for the backend, and Python for machine learning.

Research is part of the build, not a warm-up.

I once built the entire UI and data logic for a LeetCode Analytics platform, only to discover at the very end that LeetCode's API had paid limits. The work was wasted. Since then, feasibility and limits are validated before a single line of production code is written.

Division of work beats heroics.

At the Noesis hackathon, the problem statement changed mid-event, and our connectivity dropped right before submission. The demo broke. Instead of panicking, we instantly split roles, debugged in parallel, and presented calmly. We won first place.
